# BabulTech CRM

An internal CRM built from *BabulTech CRM — Complete Entity Relationship Diagram and Data Model* v1.0, plus a Partner Management and Commission module that the specification did not cover.

Next.js 15 (App Router) · TypeScript · PostgreSQL + Prisma · Tailwind · Auth.js

---

## 1. Review of the requirements document

The spec is genuinely good. It is coherent, it avoids enterprise object sprawl, its business rules (§13) are the right ones, and the phased delivery plan (§14.1) is sensible. I implemented it essentially as written. What follows is what I changed and why.

### 1.1 Partner management — what already existed

You asked whether partner management was missing. **Partly.** The spec already had:

| Spec element | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `Account.account_type = Partner` | §10.2 | Partners live as Accounts — the right call |
| `Partner Profile` | §10.2 | `partner_type`, `commission_percent`, `territory`, agreement dates, manager |
| `Opportunity.partner_profile_id` | §10.2 | Marks the "influencing partner" on a deal |
| Business rule | §13 | "Partner Profile is allowed only when `Account.account_type = Partner`" |

### 1.2 Partner management — what was missing

Three real gaps, all of which I closed.

**a. An individual partner had nowhere to live.** `Partner Profile.account_id` was mandatory, and so was `Contact.account_id`. A freelance referrer with no company could not be represented without inventing a fake Account. You explicitly asked for this case.

*Fix:* `Contact.accountId` is now nullable, and the spec's `Partner Profile` is replaced by a standalone **`Partner`** entity that links to **either** an Account (`kind = COMPANY`) **or** a Contact (`kind = INDIVIDUAL`) — never both, never neither. A database `CHECK` constraint plus a trigger enforce that, and the trigger also enforces the spec's §13 rule that a linked Account must be of type `PARTNER`.

**b. There was no commission engine at all.** `commission_percent` was a static number with nothing reading it. Nothing calculated what was earned, nothing tracked what was owed, nothing paid it, and nothing appeared in any financial report.

*Fix:* four new entities forming an auditable ledger — see §1.3.

**c. One deal could only have one partner.** `partner_profile_id` is a single FK. In practice a deal is often sourced by one partner and delivered by another, and both expect paying.

*Fix:* **`OpportunityPartner`**, an N:M junction carrying `role` (Sourced / Influenced / Resold / Delivered), `revenueSharePercent`, a per-deal rate override, and deal-registration dates. A trigger prevents the shares on a deal exceeding 100%.

### 1.3 The commission model

```
Partner ──< OpportunityPartner >── Opportunity
   │              │
   │              └─ role, revenue share %, rate override, registration expiry
   │
   ├─ CommissionPlan ──< CommissionTier      (flat / tiered / fixed)
   │
   └──< CommissionRecord >── CommissionPayout ──> FinancialTransaction
             │
             └─ ACCRUED → PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED → PAYABLE → PAID
                                                     └─ CLAWED_BACK (reversing entry)
```

Design decisions worth flagging:

- **Default trigger is `ON_PAYMENT_RECEIVED`, not "deal won."** You should not owe commission on money you have not collected. Plans can also pay on close-won or on invoice-sent where you have negotiated that.
- **Commission is calculated on revenue net of tax.** You don't pay a partner a cut of the sales tax you collect for the state.
- **Partial payment earns partial commission.** A payment allocated across invoices accrues pro rata.
- **Plans are snapshotted at deal registration.** `OpportunityPartner.commissionPlanId` freezes the terms, so editing a plan next year cannot retroactively rewrite what was earned on last year's deals.
- **Tiers apply progressively.** 1.5M against "0–1M @ 5%, 1M+ @ 8%" earns 50,000 + 40,000 = 90,000, not a flat 8%.
- **Nothing is ever deleted.** A clawback posts a negative reversing record and marks the original — the ledger stays immutable, consistent with §13 Audit and Soft deletion.
- **Withholding tax is deducted at payout.** Relevant for Pakistani partner payments; `netPayableAmount` is what actually leaves the bank.
- **Paying a payout posts a `FinancialTransaction`**, so partner commission shows up in the cash register and in project profitability as a cost of sale.

### 1.4 Other changes I made to the spec

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| `ApprovalStep` added | §10.6 `Approval Request` has a single `current_approver_id`. That models a one-hop approval; the moment you need "manager then finance," you need steps. |
| `NumberSequence` table added | §1.1 requires human-readable numbers "generated by controlled sequences" but never modelled the sequence. Added with row-level atomic increment. |
| `Product.standardCost`, `commissionPercent`, `commissionable` | Project Profitability (§11) needs cost, not just price. Hardware you resell at 2% margin should not pay 10% commission — hence the per-product exclusion. |
| `Currency.exchangeRate`, `isBase` | §11 aggregates money across currencies but had no rate to convert with. |
| `Invoice.writeOffAmount`, `Payment.unallocatedAmount` | Appendix A lists a `Written Off` invoice status but no field to record the amount. Unapplied cash is a real AR concept you will need. |
| `Case.firstRespondedAt`, `slaBreached`, `reopenCount`, `satisfactionScore` | §10.3 stores the SLA *deadline* but not whether it was actually met — so you could never report on SLA attainment. |
| `Contact.accountId` nullable | See §1.2a. |
| `Milestone.billingAmount`, `invoicedAt` | §13 milestone billing needs to know whether a milestone has already been invoiced, or you double-bill. |
| `SecurityRole.dataScope` | §14.2 mandates row-level authorization by role/team/ownership/department, but no field carried the scope. |
| Loss reason, case assignment, time-log context, quote acceptance | §13 rules moved into actual DB constraints rather than living only in prose. |

### 1.5 Things I did *not* change, and why

- **No double-entry ledger.** §14.2 says to add one only if you intend to replace your accounting system. Do not.
- **Polymorphic parents kept for Activity / Email / Note / Document / Approval / Audit only.** Exactly as §1.1 specifies. Everything transactional uses explicit foreign keys.
- **AR / AP / revenue / profitability stay as views**, not maintained tables — §11's instruction, and the right one.

### 1.6 What I would raise before you go live

1. **Currency conversion is modelled but not implemented.** `Currency.exchangeRate` is a single current rate. If you sell in USD and report in PKR, you need dated rates and a decision on whether to book at invoice date or payment date. That is a real finance decision, not a technical one.
2. **Partner portal.** Partners will ask to see their own pipeline and commission statement. The data model supports it; it needs an external-facing auth path, and it's a bigger security surface than the internal app.
3. **Document storage.** `Document` stores metadata and a `storageUrl` per §13. You still need to pick object storage (S3 / Supabase / Azure Blob) and wire signed URLs.
4. **Email ingestion.** The `Email` entity is modelled; ingesting from Microsoft 365 into it is a background-job build of its own.
5. **SLA business-hours arithmetic.** `BusinessHours.weeklySchedule` and `SlaTimerEvent` are in place, but the function that walks a working calendar to compute an SLA deadline is not yet written. It is fiddly and deserves its own tests.

## 2. Getting it running

### Prerequisites

Node 22 and PostgreSQL 16, both via Homebrew. On Apple Silicon `node@22` is
keg-only, so it needs to be on your PATH explicitly:

```bash
/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"
brew install node@22 postgresql@16
brew services start postgresql@16
echo 'export PATH="/opt/homebrew/opt/node@22/bin:/opt/homebrew/opt/postgresql@16/bin:$PATH"' >> ~/.zprofile
source ~/.zprofile
```

### Setup

```bash
cd ~/babultech-crm

npm install

createdb babultech_crm

cp .env.example .env
# Homebrew's PostgreSQL has no "postgres" role — the role is your macOS
# username, with no password. So DATABASE_URL looks like:
#   postgresql://<your-username>@localhost:5432/babultech_crm?schema=public
# Then generate a secret:
#   openssl rand -base64 32   →  AUTH_SECRET

npx prisma migrate dev --name init      # build the schema
# Pass the database name, not $DATABASE_URL — psql rejects the ?schema= param.
psql babultech_crm -f prisma/sql/01_constraints.sql   # checks + triggers
psql babultech_crm -f prisma/sql/02_views.sql         # reporting views
npm run db:seed                          # reference + demo data

npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:3000/login.

> The constraints and views are plain SQL rather than Prisma models because Prisma's schema language cannot express `CHECK`, triggers, partial unique indexes or views. Re-run both files after any `prisma migrate`.

## 3. Layout

```
prisma/
  schema.prisma          all 55 spec entities + 6 partner/commission entities
  seed.ts                reference data + the demo scenario above
  sql/01_constraints.sql CHECK constraints and triggers Prisma can't express
  sql/02_views.sql       §11 calculated views + partner performance + liability

src/lib/
  prisma.ts              singleton client
  auth.ts                Auth.js credentials provider
  authz.ts               permissions + row-level scope filters   ← read this one
  audit.ts               field-level change history
  numbering.ts           controlled sequences for record numbers
  utils.ts               Decimal-safe money formatting

src/server/              server actions (all mutations)
  commission-engine.ts   accrual, tiering, clawback              ← and this one
  commissions.ts         approve / batch / pay workflow
  partners.ts            partner CRUD, deal attachment
  opportunities.ts       pipeline + stage rules
  crm.ts                 accounts, contacts, leads, campaigns, products

src/app/(app)/           the application; every page is a server component
src/components/          UI kit and app shell
```

### Authorization

Two layers, and only one of them counts:

- `middleware.ts` — a cookie presence check to bounce anonymous traffic. Cannot be trusted; it runs on the Edge runtime and cannot reach the database.
- **`requireUser()` / `requirePermission()` / `scopeFilter()` in `src/lib/authz.ts`** — the real gate. Every page and every server action calls one of them, and list queries are narrowed by `dataScope` (OWN / TEAM / DEPARTMENT / ALL) before the database is touched. This is §14.2's "do not rely only on front-end hiding."

## 4. Delivery status

Following the spec's own phasing (§14.1):

"Working UI" below means **you can create and edit records**, not just look at them.

| Phase | Schema | List | Detail | Create / edit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Core CRM | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Accounts, Contacts, Leads (+ conversion), Opportunities (+ line items, stage moves) |
| **Partners** (new) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Both partner kinds, deal splits, commission ledger, approvals, payouts, clawbacks |
| 2 — Support | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Cases against a customer contact, SLA deadlines, first response, resolution |
| 3 — Professional Services | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Projects, phases, milestones, tasks, team/allocation, RAID, timesheets, approvals, utilisation |
| 2 — Commercial | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Quote builder with versioning and accept/reject, contracts |
| 4 — Finance | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Invoices, milestone and time billing runs, payments and cash application |
| 1 — Reference data | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ Campaigns and Products are read-only |
| 5 — Optimisation | Partial | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ Approvals engine, portal, integrations, forecasting |

The commercial and finance write paths enforce their own rules: a sent quote is
revised rather than edited and only one version per deal can be accepted; an
issued invoice is not editable and is written off rather than amended; payments
cannot overpay an invoice, settle a draft one, or cross currencies; and both
billing runs are safe to re-run without duplicating.

Every record referenced by a foreign key links through to that record — accounts,
contacts, leads, opportunities, quotes, contracts, cases, projects, invoices,
products, campaigns and partners all have a page, and `entityHref()` in
`src/lib/utils.ts` resolves the polymorphic (entityType, entityId) pairs used by
Activity, Note, Document and Audit.

Every module's **data model is complete** — the remaining work is screens and server actions, not migrations.

The gap splits in two. Campaigns, Products and Activities need only forms; the write logic is
shallow. Quotations, Invoices, Cases and Projects need their action layer built first, and each
carries real business logic (quote line items and totals, invoice allocation, SLA timers,
milestone billing).

### What has actually been run

Phase 1 and the partner module are compiled, type-checked and exercised against the database:
account create, opportunity create with line items (discount maths verified), and lead conversion
including automatic partner attribution. Everything else has been type-checked and renders, but
its write paths do not exist to test.
